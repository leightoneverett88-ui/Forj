Walking

Above the reservoirs the valley stops being pretty and starts being serious. Blanket bog, peat hags, cotton grass, and cairns older than anything written down about them. There are no waymarks up here and often no path at all — which is the reason to go.

We run guided days on the tops and hand out route cards for the rest. Either way, tell us where you're going and when you're back. We'll leave the kettle on and come looking if you don't.

Drygarn Fawr

The big one. Up onto the Cambrian watershed to the twin beehive cairns, then back down the Claerwen side. Bog to the knee in places. Guided, small groups.

Exploring

The valley below the waterline is a Victorian engineering project that happens to be beautiful. Five dams in cut stone, a railway that only ever carried its own construction, and three drowned villages under the surface of Caban Coch. In a dry summer the walls of the old farms come back up.

Half a mile upstream sits Nant-y-Gro: a small dam that Barnes Wallis blew apart in 1942 to prove the bouncing bomb would work. It's still in pieces. You can walk to it in twenty minutes and nobody will be there.

On the water

Seventy thousand million litres of it, held between hills, flat as glass most mornings and unrecognisable by four in the afternoon. We paddle the permitted stretches under the estate's access agreement — which keeps the crowds off and the water clean.

It is cold. It stays cold in August. We'll put you in a wetsuit, teach you what to do when you come off, and go out with you until you'd rather we didn't.

Paddleboard

Two hours from the jetty, usually at first light because that's when the water is still. Boards, leashes, buoyancy aids and a full change of clothes waiting.

2 hrs · from £38 · age 10+

The sky

The whole estate is an International Dark Sky Park, and the lodge has no outside lighting beyond a single shielded lamp on the boot room door. On a clear moonless night the Milky Way casts a shadow off the jetty rail. Most guests didn't believe that until they stood on it.

There's a 10-inch reflector in the shed, free to borrow, and a laminated card of what's overhead this month pinned inside the door.

252 mThe lodge

Where you sleep

A stone farmhouse, a converted barn, and Y Caban itself — the timber lodge on the shore that people book a year out. Twenty-eight beds between them, run by four people who live here. Breakfast at seven. Soup and bread left out for anyone back late. No televisions, patchy signal, and a woodstove that does most of the work from October.

The drying room takes forty sets of kit and turns them around overnight. We are unreasonably proud of it.
